# Make something, then let a friend try it

A suggested 60-minute activity for pairs, with an adult available to help. Adapt the pace to the children; finishing every step is optional.

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0–8 minutes | Each child describes a game they enjoy and one thing they would like to make. |
| 8–15 minutes | Try a maze or the animal garden. Notice what happens after one action. |
| 15–25 minutes | Draw a small map or describe an animal interaction. Choose one rule that a friend can test. |
| 25–40 minutes | Build or change that part. An adult can type a request approved by the child or help edit the example. |
| 40–50 minutes | Swap roles. One child explains a rule; the other tries it without the explainer taking over the mouse. Find one unclear detail and explain it again. |
| 50–57 minutes | Make one change in response to the test. Try the same action again and compare the result. |
| 57–60 minutes | Show the change and say what you would like to try next, if anything. |

## Small, testable ideas

- **Maze:** put the ladder beside a wall and the door on a connected section of the wall top. Can a friend collect the items, climb up and reach it?
- **Animals:** rename a pet, save, then reload. Does the new name stay? Feed it or play ball and describe the visible response.
- **AI requests:** say what you did, what happened, and what you wanted to happen. A platform message saying “fixed” is a reason to test again, not proof that the behaviour is correct.

## What to record

For each child, keep one request, one observation from a friend, and one before-and-after result. Note whether the child worked independently, needed a hint, or asked an adult to perform an operation. These describe the support provided, not a fixed judgement of ability.

An explanation followed by a friend's successful test can provide evidence of understanding. One session cannot establish lasting learning gains, creativity gains or a lasting interest.
